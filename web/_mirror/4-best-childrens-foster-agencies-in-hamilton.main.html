4 Best Children’s Foster Agencies in Hamilton

Choosing a foster agency is one of the most important decisions you’ll make as a prospective foster parent, because the agency you work with will shape your experience, your training, and the support you receive.

This article explains what foster agencies do and highlights trusted foster agencies in Hamilton to help you decide where to take the next step.

What Is a Foster Agency and What Does It Do?

A foster agency is responsible for recruiting, training, licensing, and supporting foster parents while ensuring children are placed in safe and appropriate homes. In Ontario, foster agencies work under provincial child welfare legislation and collaborate closely with Children’s Aid Societies to meet the needs of children and youth who cannot live safely with their families.

Beyond licensing, foster agencies provide ongoing guidance that prepares you for the realities of fostering, including trauma-informed care, behaviour support, and collaboration with caseworkers and schools. As you move through the process, your agency becomes your primary source of training, 24/7 support, and advocacy, helping you focus on what matters most: providing stability and care to a child in your home.

Why We Made This List

We created this list to give you clear, practical information that helps you take the next step toward becoming a foster parent in Hamilton. Many people feel called to foster but struggle to understand the differences between agencies or where to begin, and this guide is meant to remove that uncertainty.

At the same time, the need for foster parents in Ontario continues to grow, with more children and youth requiring safe, supportive homes. By highlighting established foster agencies in Hamilton, we aim to connect willing caregivers with organizations that can support them effectively and help meet this urgent community need.

How Do You Sign Up to Become a Foster Parent in Hamilton?

Becoming a foster parent in Hamilton starts with choosing a licensed foster care agency or Children’s Aid Society and reaching out to learn more about their process. Once you make contact, you can expect an initial conversation to discuss your interest, availability, and the types of placements you may be suited for. This early step helps ensure there is a good fit between you, the agency, and the needs of children in care.

From there, the process typically includes training, a home study, background checks, and ongoing preparation. While the steps are thorough, they are designed to support you, not screen you out. Throughout the process, agencies provide guidance so you understand expectations, feel prepared for the realities of fostering, and have access to support once a child is placed in your home.
