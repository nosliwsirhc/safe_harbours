Choosing a foster agency is one of the most important decisions you will make as a prospective foster parent, because the right agency shapes your experience, your support system, and the quality of care a child receives in your home.

A good agency does more than place children; it prepares you, supports you, and stands beside you when fostering becomes complex.

## **What Does a Foster Agency Do?**

A foster agency is responsible for preparing you to foster and supporting you once a child is placed in your home. The agency completes your home study, delivers required training such as PRIDE and trauma-informed care, and assesses your household to understand your strengths, limits, and capacity. This process is not about passing or failing; it is about finding the right match between a child and a family.

Once you are approved, the agency stays actively involved. You are assigned a case manager who checks in regularly, visits your home at least once a month, and helps you navigate schools, medical care, documentation, and child welfare expectations. In practice, a foster agency acts as both a safeguard for children and a steady guide for foster parents.

## **Should You Sign Up with Children’s Aid Services (CAS) or Through a Foster Agency?**

In Ontario, Children’s Aid Society investigates child protection concerns and holds legal responsibility for children in care. You can foster directly through CAS, but many people choose to foster through an agency instead because of the added layer of support.

When you foster through an agency, you are not managing the system alone. Agencies often have smaller caseloads, respond more quickly, and provide clearer guidance when issues arise. They also tend to be more selective with placements, especially for first-time foster parents, which helps prevent overwhelm and burnout. For many families, this structure makes fostering more manageable and sustainable over time.

## **What Support Does a Good Foster Agency Provide?**

A good foster agency provides layered, hands-on support that continues for as long as a child is in your home, because fostering involves complex needs, changing situations, and decisions that should never be handled in isolation. Support starts before approval with structured training in trauma-informed care, PRIDE, cultural competency, and crisis response, so you understand both the child welfare system and the realities of day-to-day fostering.

Once a placement begins, support becomes consistent and practical. A dedicated case manager maintains regular contact, visits your home at least monthly, and acts as your primary guide through school issues, medical care, family visits, documentation, and coordination with Children’s Aid. When a child’s needs increase, a strong agency arranges additional help such as child and youth workers, therapy services, respite care, and after-hours crisis support. The purpose of this support is simple: to help you provide stable care without burning out or feeling unprepared.

**What a good agency should provide:**

1.  Mandatory pre-service training, including PRIDE and trauma-informed care
2.  Ongoing training that adapts as your experience grows
3.  A dedicated case manager with regular, predictable contact
4.  Monthly in-home visits and ongoing check-ins with foster parents and children
5.  Advocacy and guidance when working with Children’s Aid, schools, and service providers
6.  Access to respite care to prevent burnout
7.  Crisis support outside regular business hours
8.  Coordination of additional services such as therapy or in-home workers when needed

## **How to Choose a Foster Agency**

Choosing a foster agency means evaluating how the agency operates when fostering becomes difficult, not just how they present themselves during intake. A strong agency is transparent about expectations, honest about children’s needs, and clear about the level of commitment required from foster parents. During early conversations, pay attention to whether staff encourage questions and acknowledge challenges instead of minimizing them.

You should also assess how the agency approaches matching and placement decisions. Good agencies respect your limits, support your right to decline placements that are not a good fit, and prioritize stability over speed. They view foster parents as long-term partners, not temporary solutions, and they take responsibility for supporting both the child and your household when circumstances change.

**How to evaluate a foster agency:**

1.  Clear explanation of the home study and approval process
2.  Honest discussion of the types of children placed and their needs
3.  Transparency around expectations, responsibilities, and boundaries
4.  Defined placement matching process that respects your preferences
5.  Clear procedures for respite, placement challenges, and emergencies
6.  Consistent communication and accessible staff
7.  Willingness to advocate for foster parents with Children’s Aid
8.  Evidence of long-term involvement after a child is placed
